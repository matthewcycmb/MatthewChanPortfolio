On September 1st, I launched Konvo on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7500010677047246848/) from an airport in Korea, right before my flight back to Vancouver. When I arrived, I opened LinkedIn and was surprised to see that the post had over 1,000 likes and 99+ connections. That one post reached around 200,000 impressions in the first three days.

I basically copied the structure of another LinkedIn creator whose app went viral and plugged mine in lol. I didn’t expect that much traction from a problem I was solving for myself.

I got my first paying users that month, but people were getting stuck on “Loading your plans.” I didn’t realise how urgent it was after the first report. When around ten people reported the same problem, I gave their messages and screenshots to Codex to help figure out what was wrong.

Codex found that the paywall was waiting for three products, including a lifetime purchase Apple hadn’t approved. My local StoreKit test file included it, which hid the problem on my phone. With Codex’s help, I changed build 82 to wait only for products I was actually selling and requested an expedited review that day. I contacted everyone who’d reported the problem, and most confirmed it worked.

Seeing people actually pay for Konvo made me want to keep working on it instead of moving on to another project.

I also started posting about Konvo on [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/matthewasherelol/) and TikTok. By September 27, I’d posted around 14 trial Reels; two reached 20,000 and 30,000 views. My Instagram posts about Konvo had around 80,000 views in total by then.

I tried studying clips with a text hook and demo, and videos where I explained Konvo straight to the camera. I noticed download spikes after the talking-head posts. That’s a reason to keep testing them, but it doesn’t tell me which posts the downloads came from. By September 27, I was planning to make roughly 80% of my videos talking-head posts and keep testing the studying clips too.

As of September 21, 2026, Konvo had 1,500 downloads and US$1,066 in gross revenue.

Of 871 people whose first tracked app open was September 1–19, 60 had another recorded app open on Day 7. Among the 141 people whose first tracked chat open was in that same period, 37 had another recorded chat open on Day 7. This uses data through September 26. Tracking may miss some reopenings, and a recorded chat open doesn’t show whether someone successfully sent a message or scrolled less.

I’d just started school, so I was mainly posting low-effort Reels. Most of the traction I’d got so far was from that one LinkedIn post, and I felt like I was plateauing. I was doubting myself, like, is this really it?

My parents told me to make a video that was simple and straight to the point. Then at 7 pm on September 28, I posted a [trial Reel explaining my age and my app](https://www.instagram.com/p/Dd0TQrPo5mZ/). It got 2 million views in less than 24 hours. I used InstantDM so when people commented “app”, they’d get sent the download link.

Building my first app took up almost my entire summer break. I already knew how to edit in CapCut from posting for the past year, but I was a complete beginner at the app side of things. I had to learn how to navigate App Store Connect, upload and submit the app, set up the paywall, and on top of that, get users.

On September 30, my story about building Konvo was published on [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/konvoinstall-com).
